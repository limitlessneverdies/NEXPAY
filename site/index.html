A payment system built for no-signal.

Every note is cryptographically signed, bound to one recipient, capped, expiring — and verified offline before it's ever accepted.

✍️ Signed notes

ECDSA P-256 signatures over amount, recipient, expiry and issuer. Tampering is detected on the spot — even offline.

🎯 One recipient only

Each note is locked to a single wallet. Redirecting it to someone else fails verification.

🔂 Single-use by design

Notes can't be split or re-spent. First valid redemption settles; replays return the original result.

📷 QR exchange

Signed codes as QR. Camera permission is requested only for scanning.

🔵 Bluetooth

Secure RFCOMM delivery with pairing, acknowledgement signing and reconnect support.

📶 Wi-Fi Direct

Fast phone-to-phone transfer with no router and no mobile data.

📲 NFC tap-tap

Two-tap exchange between HCE-capable phones: read once, deliver once.

🔐 Keystore-held keys

Signing keys live in the Android Keystore. Local state is AES-GCM encrypted.

⏳ Expiry + escrow

Notes expire in 24 hours, reserved funds sit in escrow — never double-counted online.

How a payment flies.

1
While online, reserve an exact-amount note. Funds move to escrow.
2
Go offline. The receiver shows their signed code; you sign a note for them.
3
Deliver by QR, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Direct or NFC. They verify and save a pending receipt.
4
Reconnect and sync. The ledger settles it once — then it's spendable.

Security, stated plainly.

  • Signed, not trusted. Every note is verified for issuer, owner signature, recipient binding, amount and expiry — by the receiver, before acceptance.
  • Exactly-once settlement. Operation IDs make retries safe and replays worthless.
  • Server-side authority. Balances, escrow and issuer keys live on the server. The app holds no secrets worth stealing.
  • Known limit: a compromised phone can sign one note for two people offline — only the first settles. Demonstrated by an automated test, shown in the UI.
  • Test credit only. The network runs on Rs 1,000 trial grants, not real NPR. No bank, no cash-out — by design, for now.
